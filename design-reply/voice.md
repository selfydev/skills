## Core (every register)

These are generic and work for most people. Edit any that don't fit you.

- Answer what was asked. One reason, not the whole case. Nuance goes in the Assessment, not the draft.
- Length tracks the question. A "wdyt?" gets one line. A design question in chat gets 2-3 short lines. Only a real debate earns 4-6 sentences. Your real average message length: `<fill in, e.g. about 15 words>`.
- Punctuation you never use: `<fill in, e.g. em dashes and en dashes>`. Use commas, full stops and new lines instead.
- How you give an opinion: `<fill in, e.g. hedged ("i think", "feels like", "would be careful of") or direct>`. Never verdict-speak like "my lean is", "the right call here is", "the issue here is".
- Say what users would experience, not the mechanism ("shows up when it shouldn't", not "fires on unrelated actions").
- Name the actual thing. "the save flow", not "the save one". Real feature names, ticket numbers.
- Never: reassurance filler ("nothing dramatic i promise"), gossip framing ("the theory going round"), pre-framing ("quick one", "quick update on"), proposing a call unless they raised one, justifying your own standing, casting yourself as the complainant when someone else raised it.
- Don't repeat a distinctive word in a short message.
- Same context depth for every recipient of the same situation. Never a trimmed summary for person two.
- Don't re-explain history the other person lived through. Skip the backstory and make the ask.
- Don't state something happened (a decision, a conversation) unless it did. Say it as the plan or the assumption it is.
- End on the real question if there is one. "wdyt" usually means "what should we do", not yes or no.

## Register 3: external (people outside your team)

Plain, polite, brief. This is the one register where a greeting and a sign-off belong.

- Greeting and sign-off: `<fill in, e.g. "Hi <name>," and "Best, <your name>">`
- 2-3 short paragraphs. "Thank you for getting in touch" is fine here.
- Express interest and any one concern. No metrics, percentages or achievement lists unless they asked for evidence.
- Describe your work in plain past-tense facts, never as a method or philosophy. Not "I decide what we track before anything gets designed", but "most of my work now is UX, including working out what to track and testing changes."
- One question at most, the one that decides whether it goes ahead.
- A follow-up states the new fact and stops. No asking them to look, no thanks in advance, no re-pitch of what they can already read. Warm, not cold.
- Friendly asks to people you already know use the chat register, even on email or social platforms.
